HOW TO USE IT

If your breath isn't being heard

What to try, in order, when you see "No exhale was heard". Distance, the room, the difference between devices, and carrying on without the microphone.

6 min read

If the app can't hear your breath, the session doesn't become useless — but the score becomes meaningless. The cause is usually one of a few things. Let's go through them in order.

1. Distance

This is the most common cause. Hold the device 20-30 cm from your mouth and nose.

If you laid it flat on a table, the microphone is most likely facing sideways rather than up. Propping the phone against a book, or holding it, makes a clear difference.

Know where the microphone is: on an iPhone it is on the bottom edge, next to the charging port. If your hand covers it, nothing is heard.

2. The room

There is a three-second measurement at the start of a session — while the screen says "Measuring the room…", don't talk and don't move. A sound in that moment is taken for room noise, and the threshold stays wrong for the whole session.

A television, air conditioning, a fan, an open window: all of them raise the threshold. A quiet room is best.

3. Your exhale really being very quiet

Some techniques are quiet by nature. A breath out through a single nostril is the quietest of all; Coherent and Deep and Slow are low too.

The app knows this and uses a different threshold for each technique. But it varies from person to person as well: some people's nasal exhale really is very quiet.

The fix is not to force the breath. Pushing to make the exhale stronger both spoils the technique and lowers the "breath volume" measurement. Bring the device closer instead.

4. If you are using a tablet

On a tablet the microphone array is wide and the input level comes out noticeably lower than on a phone. In one tablet recording, the highest point of the exhale was far below even the quietest of the phone recordings.

On a tablet the app picks the microphone source nearest your mouth, but the gap doesn't close completely. On a tablet you need to hold it closer.

5. Look at what the report says

Under Detailed report there is an "Exhale peak level" line: something like "20 dB above the floor".

If that difference is under 10 dB, the sound really is too weak — work on distance and the room. If it is 20 dB or more, the sound is enough and the problem may be elsewhere (timing, for instance).

If the line isn't there at all, no measurable exhale was found in that session.

6. Microphone permission

If Breath detection (microphone) is off in Settings, nothing is measured. If you denied the permission in iOS's Settings app, the app says so and shows a button that takes you straight there.

You can do a session without the microphone

If none of this helps, or you simply don't feel like it, carry on without the microphone: Continue without the microphone when the session starts, or turn the microphone off entirely in Settings.

In a session without the microphone no score is worked out, but the circle, the rhythm, the haptics, your streak, milestones and programs all work exactly the same. The report says it was a "session without the microphone", and the session still counts towards your streak.

One more thing

If the app doesn't hear your exhale for a while during a session, it shows a short warning. That warning uses the same threshold as the measurement: it won't say "not being heard" on screen and "heard" in the report. If you are seeing the warning, it really can't measure it.
