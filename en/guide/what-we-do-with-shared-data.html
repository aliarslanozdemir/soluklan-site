BEHIND THE SCENES

What we do with the data you share

If you turned on the anonymous session summary and audio samples, what those records come to us for, what happens to each one, and what becomes of them in the end.

6 min read

There are two separate switches under Settings → Privacy: the anonymous usage summary and the occasional audio sample. Both arrive off, both are asked about separately, and both can be turned off whenever you like. If you didn't turn them on, nothing in this article happens on your device.

If you did, here is what becomes of a record, step by step.

What arrives

With the anonymous summary on, this is what comes when a session ends: the technique, the length, the score and the report's numerical values — exhales heard, average exhale length, rhythm match, peak sound level, noise floor and so on. Alongside them, the device type and the app version.

What doesn't come: your name, your email address, your identity, your location, your health answers from the intake, and your age. These never leave your device.

The audio sample is a separate permission. With it on, the audio of at most five sessions a day is sent in compressed form and deleted from the device the moment it is sent. With the permission off, no audio is written to disk in any session.

The feedback question that comes up now and then

With anonymous sharing on, a short question appears at the end of a session now and then: a score out of ten, followed by one more question — depending on your score, "what didn't you like", "what could we improve" or "what did you like most". The second question names the technique you did.

You can close it without giving a score. You can also give the score and leave without writing anything: the button at the bottom then says Send the score only, so not writing doesn't throw the score away.

What is sent: the score you gave, the text you wrote, the technique, the length, the session score, whether the session was completed, whether the microphone was used, the app version and the date. Alongside them, the anonymous identifier above. What isn't sent is the same here: name, email address, identity, location, health answers, age.

We read what you write — it is the only place that says what the numbers can't. So don't put your name, your email address or anything identifying in the box; if you want a reply, the right address is the support page.

If you skip it, the app doesn't ask again for a while, and that wait grows with each skip; after a few skips it stops asking altogether. You are also asked once when you finish or abandon a program — the abandon question asks for no score, only the reason.

If you didn't turn anonymous sharing on, this question never appears.

What we do

As records build up we ask one question: is what the app says true?

We look for contradictions. Numbers that don't agree within the same session are the quickest clue. A report saying "every exhale was heard" while the same session shows an average exhale length of zero means something is wrong somewhere. We scan for these inconsistencies between measurements.

We look at the distribution. When a value clusters outside what we expect, that is usually a threshold error. A real example: exhale peak levels were coming out systematically low on one family of devices — it turned out that on tablets the microphone sits on the device's far edge.

We compare against the audio sample. When we have both the sound itself and the numbers the app produced for it, we can measure whether the detection is genuinely right: is there really an exhale where the app said "exhale", and did it miss any breaths. That is the audio sample's only job.

We read the feedback by hand. Scores on their own don't get past "good/bad"; what is useful is the sentence next to them. When the same complaint comes from several people, it goes on the list. This article came out of exactly that.

We fix it and test again. For every error we find, a test is written first — one that reproduces the error, based on the real recording we have. Then the fix is made. If the test passes, the change goes into the next release; if it doesn't, the fix was wrong.

What we don't do

No ads, no tracking, no third-party analytics tools. The records aren't sold, aren't handed to an ad network, and aren't transferred to another company. They aren't matched to an identity: we hold no link showing who sent which record.

The records don't sit in your iCloud but in the app's shared database, and they are used only for the work described above.

Changing your mind

Both switches are turned off under Settings → Privacy. From the moment you turn them off, nothing more is sent.

If you want what you sent earlier deleted, write to info@soluklan.app. Because the records aren't tied to an identity, we may ask for the dates you sent them so we can find yours.
