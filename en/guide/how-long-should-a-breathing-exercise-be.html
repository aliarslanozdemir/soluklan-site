How long should a breathing exercise be

The short answer: three to five minutes a day. The long answer is about where length helps and where it stops paying off.

4 min read

The short answer: three to five minutes a day. If you want the long answer, here is where length helps and where it stops paying off.

Why three to five minutes

In the study people talk about most, participants practised five minutes a day for a month; that is where the measured difference came from. So "five minutes" is not a number someone picked — it is a tested dose. One minute is often not even enough for the rhythm to settle: the first half minute goes into finding the pace.

Why that number keeps coming up, and how we read the evidence behind it, is a separate piece.

Why longer isn't linear

Twenty minutes does not give four times the benefit of five. There are two reasons. First, once the pace has settled, every extra minute repeats the same thing. Second, a long session becomes a reason to quit: on the day you can't spare twenty minutes you do nothing, while three minutes can always be found.

We wrote about the habit side in the first thirty days.

What changes with the technique

  • Slow techniques like coherent breathing and Deep and Slow carry a long session well; five to ten minutes is comfortable.
  • Techniques with holds (4-7-8 Breathing, Box Breathing) are counted in rounds, not minutes. Four rounds is enough for most people; if you feel light-headed, cut the rounds.
  • Stimulating techniques stay short. Kapalabhati is measured in rounds and the app puts a ceiling on it.

How the app picks a length

The length slider is clipped per technique: five minutes at most for one, one minute for a stimulating one. Your answers in the intake also move the suggested range — which technique is suggested when depends on it. If you set up a program, the program takes over the length and raises it day by day: Setting up a program and keeping it.

Does length affect the score

Not directly. The score looks at completion: did you finish the length you chose. A full three-minute session scores higher than a ten-minute one abandoned halfway. How the score is built is a separate piece.

A practical rule

Pick three minutes when you start the day. If you finish thinking "I could have done a bit more", do four tomorrow. Raise the length only on days you finish; lower it on days you don't. If you have never read how to do a breathing exercise, start there.
